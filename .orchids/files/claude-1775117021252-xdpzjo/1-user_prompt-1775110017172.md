I’ll structure this cleanly so it’s implementable, explainable to sponsors, and safe from abuse
  LOCAL LEARNING RESERVE
Turning 9% (15%-6%) Sponsor Percentage into Area-Based Support
________________________________________
1. Core Idea (Simple Truth)
Value generated in an area should partially stay in that area.
Instead of sponsor money disappearing into a global pool, a portion becomes a Local Learning Reserve (LLR) — a ring-fenced savings account tied to the geographic origin of learning cells.
This fund exists to solve immediate, practical shortages that block learning.
________________________________________
2. Where the Money Comes From
We do not increase costs.
We reallocate part of the existing Sponsor Pool.
.
________________________________________
3. What the Local Learning Reserve Can Be Used For
Only tangible, learning-enabling needs.
Approved uses (examples):
•	notebooks, pens, stationery
•	shared laptops / tablets
•	internet access support
•	basic books or readers
•	microphones / headsets
•	printer access
•	shared study space equipment
•	onboarding more learning cells in the same area
Explicitly NOT allowed:
•	cash to individuals
•	gifts unrelated to learning
•	facilitator salaries
•	sponsor branding purchases
•	political or religious use
This is infrastructure, not aid.
________________________________________
4. How the Reserve Is Structured (Important)
Each area gets:
•	a virtual wallet in the system
•	tagged by location / region
•	funded automatically per lesson or per month
•	visible on a public transparency page
No one can manually move money out.
________________________________________
5. Who Can Request Use of the Reserve
Allowed requesters:
•	Connectors (primary)
•	Facilitators (supporting)
•	Coordinators (verification)
•	Sponsors have the final decision
Required for approval:
•	short description of need
•	quantity & estimated cost
•	how it unlocks learning
•	who benefits (group-level only)
No individual student names.
________________________________________
6. Approval Process (Fast but Safe)
To avoid bureaucracy:
1.	Request submitted in app
2.	Auto-check against allowed categories
3.	Coordinator approval (or auto-approval below a threshold)
4.	Funds released to:
o	vendor, or
o	approved supplier
Never as raw cash.
________________________________________
7. Why This Strengthens the Connector Role
Connectors now:
•	don’t just bring students
•	actively improve learning conditions
•	become community problem-solvers
•	have pride and agency
This keeps connectors:
•	motivated
•	trusted
•	locally respected
Which directly supports your earlier principle:
Happy connectors keep the system alive.
________________________________________
8. Why Sponsors Will LOVE This
Sponsors get:
•	visible, real-world impact
•	area-specific outcomes
•	better stories than “we sponsored lessons”
•	measurable community improvement
Sponsor-facing language:
“Your support doesn’t just fund learning — it strengthens the environment learning comes from.”
This increases:
•	retention
•	competition
•	long-term commitment
________________________________________
9. How This Fits the Producing Model
The loop now becomes:
Sponsor funds → Learning happens →
Local reserve grows → Barriers removed →
More cells form → More learning produced
This is true circulation, not extraction.
________________________________________

11. Safeguards (Non-Negotiable)
•	capped spending per request
•	audit trail
•	no individual payouts
•	quarterly review
•	public transparency (aggregate only)
If misuse detected → reserve frozen.
________________________________________
12. One-Line Policy Text (You Can Reuse)
A portion of sponsor funding is reserved locally to remove practical barriers to learning and to grow sustainable learning capacity in the communities where students live.
________________________________________
13. Final Check
This addition:
•	does NOT complicate payments
•	does NOT create dependency
•	does NOT pressure students
•	DOES increase resilience
•	DOES increase sponsor value
•	DOES energize connectors
This is a mature system move.
