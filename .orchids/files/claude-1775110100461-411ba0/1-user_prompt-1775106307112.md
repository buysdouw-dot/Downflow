🌱 SCHOOL CURRICULUM OVERVIEW
The Inverted Education Model
Traditional school:
Content → Tests → Pressure → Identity

This school:
Mind → Self → Skills → Community → Output

We educate from the inside out.

🧠 CORE EDUCATIONAL PHILOSOPHY (Inverted Model)
Learning flows in this order:

Awareness & Curiosity
(How do I think? What interests me?)

Confidence & Expression
(Can I express myself safely?)

Skill Development
(What can I do with my body, mind, and tools?)

Value Creation
(How does my learning help others?)

Contribution & Growth
(How do I participate in the world?)

There is no rush to performance.
Competence grows naturally from confidence.

📘 CURRICULUM STRUCTURE BY AGE
The same philosophy, applied differently at each stage.

🟡 FOUNDATION STAGE
Ages 5–7
Focus: Curiosity • Safety • Play

How learning looks:

Learning through movement, stories, sound, and play
No grades, no ranking
Mistakes are normal and welcome
Key outcomes:

Confidence speaking and trying
Body awareness and coordination
Early understanding of choice and consequence
Teacher role:
Guide, encourager, observer

🟢 DEVELOPMENT STAGE
Ages 8–10
Focus: Expression • Understanding • Structure

How learning looks:

Short projects and group activities
Gentle structure introduced (not enforced)
Learners explain ideas in their own words
Key outcomes:

Clear communication
Early responsibility and independence
Understanding effort, value, and teamwork
Teacher role:
Coach, question-asker, connector

🔵 EXPRESSION STAGE
Ages 11–14
Focus: Identity • Skill • Contribution

How learning looks:

Project-based learning
Real-world simulations and role play
Learners reflect, revise, and present
Key outcomes:

Strong self-expression
Practical life skills
Confidence working with others and leading ideas
Teacher role:
Mentor, facilitator, collaborator

🧭 ASSESSMENT (INVERTED)
There are no traditional grades first.

Instead, learners are assessed on:

Engagement
Effort
Growth
Communication
Contribution
Academic benchmarks come after confidence is built, not before.

🌍 WHY THIS WORKS
Children learn how to think, not what to repeat
Skills become meaningful because they are connected to self
Motivation comes from ownership, not fear
Learners grow into creators, not consumers
